# Maize — Fall Armyworm (FAW)

**Pest:** *Spodoptera frugiperda* (Lepidoptera: Noctuidae)
**Crop:** Maize (also sorghum and other cereals)

> Starter sample document. Replace with the authoritative ICAR-NCIPM / State
> Agricultural University advisory for your region before relying on it.

## Identification

- Young larvae scrape leaf surfaces, leaving papery "windowpane" patches.
- Older larvae cause ragged holes and heavy frass (sawdust-like droppings) in
  the whorl.
- Larva has an inverted pale **Y** on the head and four dark spots in a square
  on the second-to-last body segment.

## Economic threshold (indicative)

- Early whorl stage: 5% damaged plants.
- Late whorl stage: 10% damaged plants.
- Confirm the current threshold with your state advisory before spraying.

## Integrated management

**Cultural / mechanical**
- Deep summer ploughing to expose pupae.
- Timely, uniform sowing across the area; avoid staggered planting.
- Hand-pick and destroy egg masses and larvae in small fields.
- Apply dry sand or sand + lime into the whorl of infested plants.

**Biological**
- Install pheromone traps (about 5 per acre) for monitoring and mass trapping.
- Conserve natural enemies; use recommended bio-pesticides (e.g. *Bacillus
  thuringiensis*, NPV) as per label.

**Chemical (only per registered label)**
- Use insecticides registered by CIB&RC for FAW on maize, directed into the
  whorl, at the label dose and spray volume. Rotate modes of action, observe
  the pre-harvest interval, and wear protective equipment.

## Safety

Never exceed the label dose. Spray in calm weather, keep others away from the
field during and after spraying, and store chemicals safely away from food and
children.
